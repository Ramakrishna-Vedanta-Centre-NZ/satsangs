QUESTION 1

Two ideas carry the whole of this class. Which pair are they?

A. Bhakti Yoga and Haṭha Yoga
B. Niṣkāma Karma and Seva Yoga✓ Correct answer
C. Jñāna Yoga and Rāja Yoga
D. Dharma and Artha

Niṣkāma Karma is action done wholeheartedly without craving for the result. Seva Yoga is work offered as service, seeing the Divine in the one served. See slide 1.

QUESTION 2

In the classical order of Vedantic learning, the talk at the class corresponds to which stage?

A. Renunciation (saṃnyāsa)
B. Reflection (manana)
C. Meditation (nididhyāsana)

The talk is listening (śravaṇa). The question time is reflection (manana). The two guided sittings are meditation (nididhyāsana), where the teaching begins to be absorbed. See slide 2.

QUESTION 3

Vedanta says that when everyday work becomes spiritual life, what changes?

A. The occupation: we must give up our job
B. The hours: we must work less
C. The place: we must move to a forest
D. The inner attitude, while the tasks stay the same✓ Correct answer

The cooking, the emails and the caring stay. What changes is the attitude, from “this is mine, and I must succeed” to “this is offered, and I will do my best”. The Gita is spoken on a battlefield, not in a forest. See slide 3.

Reflect: Think of a recurring task I dislike. Is the discomfort in the task, or in my resistance to it?

QUESTION 4 · MATCHING

Which knot is it? Choose one for each line.

1. “I am accomplishing this.”✓ Kartṛtva (the doer)
2. “I must get the reward.”✓ Bhoktṛtva (the enjoyer)
3. Clinging to praise and to ownership of what we do✓ Saṅga (attachment)
4. The sense of being the one who acts✓ Kartṛtva (the doer)

Kartṛtva is the sense of being the doer, bhoktṛtva the sense of being the enjoyer who must receive the reward, and saṅga is clinging to outcomes, praise and ownership. It is not action itself that binds, but this way of relating to action. Together they bring anxiety before the task and elation or disappointment after it. See slide 4.

Reflect: Which of the three knots is strongest in me this week?

QUESTION 5

Gita 2.47 begins “karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā … kadācana”. Which word completes the line, “never to its ______”?

A. kāmeṣu (the desires)
B. karmaṇi (the action)
C. phaleṣu (the fruits)✓ Correct answer
D. yajñeṣu (the offerings)

“You have a right to action alone, never to its fruits.” In Devanagari: कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन. The verse gives four instructions: act; do not claim the fruit; do not make the fruit your motive; do not drift into inaction. See slide 5.

Reflect: What is one task where I confuse caring about the work with needing the result?

QUESTION 6

In Gita 2.48, how is yoga defined?

A. Strict fasting
B. Withdrawal from all work
C. Skill in speech
D. Equanimity, being level in success and failure✓ Correct answer

Gita 2.48 says samatvaṁ yoga ucyate: evenness of mind is called yoga. Gita 2.50 adds that yoga is skill in action. Equanimity is the inner side of work and excellence the outer side. See slide 6.

Reflect: Recall a time I worked at my best. Was my mind on the task or on the result?

QUESTION 7

According to Gita 3.9, what is the one kind of work that does not bind?

A. Work done for payment
B. Work done quickly
C. Work done as an offering (yajña)✓ Correct answer
D. Work done alone

Except for work done as an offering, this world is bound by action. The same work either binds or frees, depending on whether it is offered. Krishna also says no one can remain without action even for a moment. See slide 7.

QUESTION 9

Gita 5.10 says that a person who offers all actions to Brahman is untouched, like which image?

A. A rock in a river
B. A tree in a storm
C. A candle in the wind
D. A lotus leaf in water✓ Correct answer

The lotus leaf lives in water yet stays dry. The mind can be fully engaged in the world and remain unstained. Gita 9.27 adds that even eating can be offered, and 18.46 that the work itself is the worship. See slide 9.

QUESTION 10 · MATCHING

Master or slave? Choose one for each line (Swami Vivekananda, Karma-Yoga).

1. Works under compulsion, from need, fear of loss or craving for approval✓ The slave
2. Works from inner freedom and is not owned by the work✓ The master
3. Is driven by the result and by what others will say✓ The slave
4. Can give himself fully to the work without being bound by it✓ The master

Swami Vivekananda: “You should work like a master and not as a slave; work incessantly, but do not do slave’s work.” The slave works under compulsion. The master works from inner freedom. Freedom is not the absence of work but the absence of compulsion. See slide 10.

Reflect: Where in my own work do I feel like a slave, and where like a master?

QUESTION 13

What does the teaching Śiva-jñāne jīva-sevā ask of us?

A. To serve only those who thank us
B. To serve each living being knowing it is Śiva, the Divine✓ Correct answer
C. To give money to temples

Compassion (dayā) can hide a sense of height. Service given in the knowledge that the other is Śiva keeps the giver in the position of a worshipper, which is why service becomes worship. See slide 13.

QUESTION 15

Holy Mother: “Through work alone one can remove the bondage of work, not by avoiding …”

A. Work✓ Correct answer
B. The world
C. Prayer
D. Other people

Holy Mother lived this teaching in cooking, nursing and running a household. Work purifies; avoiding work does not free us. She also taught that no one is a stranger and that the whole world is our own. See slide 15.

QUESTION 16 · MATCHING

Match each pitfall to its correction.

1. Detachment becomes indifference✓ Care fully, hold lightly
2. Hidden hope for thanks✓ Offer the work, then let it go
3. Resentment toward those served✓ See them as the Lord
4. Pride in being a giver✓ Remember that the giver is the one who is blessed

The path has five typical traps; the fifth is burnout, corrected by treating rest as part of the offering. They are not failures of character, only old habits of ego. Notice the trap without scolding yourself, smile, and return to the offering. See slide 16.

Reflect: Which of the five pitfalls is most familiar to me? What small correction can I try this week?

QUESTION 17

Which step of the five-step daily practice is “Done, offered.”?

A. Release the result✓ Correct answer
B. Morning offering
C. See the Lord in the one served
D. Remembrance in the middle of work

Releasing the result at the end of a task is Gita 2.47 in practice. The five steps are: morning offering, remembrance in the middle of work, release the result, see the Lord in the one served, and evening review. See slide 17.

Reflect: Which step is easiest for me, and which is hardest? Begin with the easiest.

QUESTION 18

What is the spirit of the evening review?

A. A strict exam of the day
B. Witness, not judge✓ Correct answer
C. A list of other people’s faults
D. A way to plan tomorrow’s work only

Vedanta describes the Self as the witness (sākṣī). The review borrows that standpoint modestly. Observation with kindness leads to learning; harsh judgement leads to discouragement. Keep it short: three lines are enough. See slide 18.

Reflect: When I look back on my day, do I observe like a witness or judge like a critic?

QUESTION 19 · MATCHING

What does each mark on the daily checklist mean?

2. A dash✓ Done partly
3. A blank✓ Not today: it is information, not failure

A blank is information, not failure. Do not aim for twelve ticks: begin with three or four items for a week, then add more. A day with a few honest ticks is worth more than a day of hurried ticks. See slide 19.

QUESTION 23

In the meditation on offering the day’s work, each task is pictured as what?

A. A flower placed at the feet of the Divine✓ Correct answer
B. A weight to put down
C. A letter to be posted
D. A seed to be planted

This is arpaṇa, offering. Then silently repeat “Whatever I do, I offer to You”, in the spirit of Gita 9.27, and rest in the stillness beneath the activity. It takes about ten minutes. See slide 23.

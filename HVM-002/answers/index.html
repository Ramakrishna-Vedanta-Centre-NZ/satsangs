QUESTION 1

A friend says: “Kali is for frightening, dark times, and the Diwali lamp is for happy times, so the two do not belong together.” How would this class answer?

A. They meet on one night, the new moon night of Kartik: the lamp is lit in the dark, not instead of it, for the Mother is one.✓ Correct answer
B. Kali belongs to Bengal and Diwali to the rest of India, so the two traditions are properly kept apart.
C. The lamp is meant to drive Kali away, so that only the gentle forms of the Mother remain in the home.
D. The friend is right, which is why Kali Puja is kept on a separate day, away from the lamps of Diwali and the joy of that night.

In 2026 Kali Puja and Diwali fall together on the new moon night of Kartik, Sunday 8 November. The class held four days before gave practices for the days leading up to that night. The point of the evening is that the dark night and the lamp are not rivals: we light the lamp in the dark, and both belong to the one Mother. See slide 3.

Reflect: What is my own dark night just now, and what small lamp could I light in it?

QUESTION 2

A friend who follows the formless Advaita says: “Worship of Kali is a lower stage, to be left behind once one knows Brahman.” Which reply is closest to Sri Ramakrishna?

A. Brahman first creates Shakti, so Shakti is a later and lesser thing that depends on Brahman for existence.
B. Brahman alone is real and Kali is a useful idea, which the wise may use for a while and then drop once the goal is reached.
C. Brahman and Shakti are identical, like fire and its power to burn: accept the one and you must accept the other.✓ Correct answer
D. The formless is for the wise and the personal Mother for beginners, so each should keep to their own stage.

“Brahman and Shakti are identical, like fire and its power to burn. If you accept the one you must accept the other.” He also said, “Kali is verily Brahman, and Brahman is verily Kali. It is one and the same Reality.” A fire that does not burn is not fire, so the Mother is not a step below the Absolute. See slide 4.

Reflect: Do I quietly treat the Mother, or the personal God, as a lower rung than the formless?

QUESTION 3 · MATCHING

Three pictures were used in the class. Which picture answers each of these four questions?

1. “Can I accept the formless Brahman and put aside the Mother?”✓ Fire and its power to burn
2. “Is the Mother at rest at one time and a different being when the world moves?”✓ The snake still and moving
3. “Is the quiet of meditation the Absolute, and the busy world something made of different stuff?”✓ The calm sea and the waves
4. “Is this Kali, with a form, really Brahman?” (Swami Vivekananda was asked this at Belur Math.)✓ The snake still and moving

The fire illustration (Sri Ramakrishna): accept one and you must accept the other. The snake (Sri Ramakrishna): “Thinking of the snake, one must think of its wriggling motion”; the same snake, at rest or moving. The sea (Swami Vivekananda, Inspired Talks): “The sea calm is the Absolute; the same sea in waves is Divine Mother.” At Belur Math on a Kali Puja night Swami Vivekananda answered the fourth question by recalling the snake moving and the snake at rest. See slide 4.

QUESTION 4

Swami Vivekananda said: “She is the sum total of the energy in the universe.” If we take that seriously, what follows for the strength, intelligence and love I find in myself?

A. They are mine, earned by my own effort, and the Mother takes over only at the point where my own run out.
B. They are Hers only when they are used for religious purposes, and are otherwise simply my personal property.
C. Only the great forces of nature are Hers, while the small powers of an individual belong to that person alone.
D. They are Hers at work in me, so there is no ground for pride, and no need to feel I must find strength alone.✓ Correct answer

He said: “Every manifestation of power in the universe is ‘Mother’”, and added, “She is life, She is intelligence, She is Love.” If every power is Hers, then my own strength, mind and love are Hers moving through me. That is the root of both humility and courage. (Swami Vivekananda, Inspired Talks, 2 July 1895.) See slide 5.

Reflect: Where did I see power, life, intelligence or love at work today? Whose power was it?

QUESTION 5

After his vision that it was She who had become everything, Sri Ramakrishna fed a cat with the food meant for the Mother. A devotee today wants to take the same truth into daily life. What is the sound way to begin?

A. Stop the formal worship, since everything is already Hers and the image no longer has any special place.
B. Take one person or creature hard to see as Hers, and for a moment today look at them as a form of the Mother.✓ Correct answer
C. Wait for a vision of one's own before treating anyone differently, since the truth must first be seen with one's own eyes.
D. Offer the food of the shrine to animals every day, since that is exactly what he did on that occasion.

“I fed a cat with the food that was to be offered to the Divine Mother. I clearly perceived that the Divine Mother Herself had become everything, even the cat.” He did not drop worship: he saw the Mother in the image and in the one at the door. For us the practice is the small, repeated looking, beginning with the person we find hardest. See slide 6.

Reflect: Which person or creature in my day am I least able to see as Hers?

QUESTION 6

Something in my life looks dark and frightening. Using Sri Ramakrishna's answer to Govinda about the Mother's colour, what is the first thing to try?

A. Come closer and know it more intimately, because things look dark mainly when we stand far away from them.✓ Correct answer
B. Paint it in a brighter colour in the mind and refuse to look directly at what is frightening in it for even a moment.
C. Step further back, so that it looks smaller, and look at it only from a distance that is comfortable.
D. Wait for the sky to change, since the colour of a thing depends on the weather and the light of the day.

“You see Her as black because you are far away from Her. Go near and you will find Her devoid of all colour.” He compared it to the water of a lake and the blue of the sky, both of which lose their colour as we come near. We do not claim every hardship dissolves on close inspection; the teaching is that distance and unfamiliarity add much of the darkness. See slide 8.

Reflect: What in my life looks dark mainly because I have been standing far away from it?

QUESTION 7

A student says: “The image shows the Mother crushing Shiva beneath Her feet.” Which answer follows Sri Ramakrishna's own words?

A. Shiva stands for the devotee's own ego, which is flattened beneath the Mother's feet as a lesson to all who look at the image.
B. Kali looks at Shiva: the picture shows the union of Purusha, who is inactive, and Prakriti, who acts with Him.✓ Correct answer
C. She is trampling a defeated enemy, which is why Shiva lies like a corpse beneath Her feet in the image, to be admired from a safe distance.
D. Shiva is dead, and She dances on because the one who lies beneath Her no longer matters at all to Her, who goes on dancing alone.

“Kali stands on the bosom of Siva; Siva lies under Her feet like a corpse; Kali looks at Siva. All this denotes the union of Purusha and Prakriti.” Read as a picture of ourselves: all our activity stands on a ground of awareness that does not act, and is never separate from it. See slide 9.

Reflect: Where do I act as though my own deepest Self were lying still and forgotten beneath my busyness?

QUESTION 8

A student learns that the garland of heads is also read as the garland of letters, each letter a Matrika or mother-power. What follows for the way we speak?

A. Speech is a distraction from the Mother, and only complete silence is holy enough to be offered to Her.
B. Only Sanskrit words carry Her power, so the rest of our everyday speech does not matter in the same way.
C. Every word we speak is made of Her power, so speech can be offered with care and is never a trivial thing.✓ Correct answer
D. The letters recall the demons She defeated, and so they have no bearing on how we should speak or what we say each day.

In the Shaiva and Tantric tradition the letters are the Matrikas, the mother-powers of sound, and creation is a phonetic manifestation of Shabda Brahman. This reading is from that tradition and from teachers of the Order, not from our three books. Sri Ramakrishna did say, “God and His name are identical.” The practical point: if speech is made of Her, then each word is either an offering or a waste of it. See slide 11.

Reflect: If every word I speak is made of Her, which of my words today would I take back?

QUESTION 9 · MATCHING

These are four worries a devotee might bring to the image. Which detail of the image answers each?

1. “Will She turn on me if I am afraid of Her?”✓ The raised open hand, “Do not fear”
2. “What must go if I am to come close?”✓ The sword, cutting bondage and ignorance
3. “What happens to my ego once I give it up?”✓ The garland of heads, worn at Her heart
4. “What becomes of my ordinary actions when they are done for Her?”✓ The girdle of hands, an ornament

Two hands destroy and two hands protect in one figure. The raised hand says do not fear. The sword cuts bondage, not people. The garland is read as egos given up and kept close to Her. The girdle is read as our hands of action, worn as Her ornament once they work for Her. Sri Ramakrishna: “Syama-Kali has a somewhat tender aspect ... She is the Dispenser of boons and the Dispeller of fear.” The symbolic readings are traditional teaching, not quotation. See slide 10.

Reflect: What would I have to let Her cut away in order to come closer to Her?

QUESTION 10

Before an important decision, someone has done their honest best and is still anxious. Which attitude matches the kitten rather than the monkey?

A. Decide that nothing is worth doing and wait without any effort, since the outcome is not in one's hands.
B. Pretend there is no worry at all, so that the Mother is not troubled by the anxiety of Her child, who ought to be calm.
C. Cry out to the Mother with real need and place the result in Her hands, as a kitten leaves all to its mother.✓ Correct answer
D. Hold on tighter by planning every possible outcome in advance, as the baby monkey clings to its mother.

“The kitten, of itself, cannot cling to its mother. It lies on the ground and cries, ‘Mew, mew!’ It leaves everything to its mother.” Sri Ramakrishna said his own nature was the kitten's. Both ways reach the mother; clinging is tiring, and crying out is honest. He also said, “I move as Thou movest me; I do as Thou makest me do”: action continues, but the weight of the result is handed over. See slide 12.

Reflect: In which part of my life am I clinging like the monkey?

QUESTION 11

A devotee reads Sri Ramakrishna's prayer: “Here is Thy knowledge, here is Thy ignorance. Take them both and grant me only pure love for Thee.” Why would he hand back even his knowledge and his virtue?

A. So that nothing, good or bad, stands between him and pure love; even virtue and knowledge can become a pride.✓ Correct answer
B. Because he was in despair and had given up the spiritual life, and the prayer was a way of saying so.
C. Because he thought virtue and knowledge worthless to other people, and wished to give them up for good and for all.
D. Because he wished to become ignorant, so as to be rid of all responsibility for what he knew and did.

He gave back virtue and vice, knowledge and ignorance, “Take them both”, and asked only for “pure love for Thy Lotus Feet, love that seeks no return.” The prayer is not against goodness; it refuses to let even goodness, or knowledge, become a possession set up between the child and the Mother. See slide 13.

Reflect: If I could ask Her for one thing only, what would it be, and is it the thing I really need?

QUESTION 12

A friend thinks surrender, “giving God the power of attorney”, means stopping effort and waiting. How would you answer from Sri Ramakrishna's words?

A. It means taking a vow of silence and inaction until the Mother answers, however long that may take and whatever else is neglected.
B. Your friend is right: when everything is given to God, nothing more is left for us to do but to wait.
C. It means making a legal will in favour of the temple, so that the Mother holds one's possessions in trust.
D. Surrender hands over the authority to decide, while one still cries to Her fervently and works as She moves one.✓ Correct answer

“Give God the power of attorney. Let Him do whatever He wants. Be like a kitten and cry to Him with a fervent heart.” A power of attorney does not end the work; it changes who holds the decision. Compare, “I move as Thou movest me; I do as Thou makest me do.” See slide 14.

QUESTION 13

A student says: “I will keep up japa only when I feel inspired; doing it otherwise would be hollow.” What would Holy Mother's teaching on japa say to this?

A. Wait until the mind is quiet, and only then begin, since practice on a restless mind is wasted effort.
B. Wait for inspiration, because japa done without feeling clears none of the mind's impurities and may do harm.
C. Practise only on festival days, when the heart is naturally open and the Mother is most easily reached by those who have prepared.
D. Keep a regular time, since no one knows when the auspicious moment will come, and the habit keeps us at the door.✓ Correct answer

She said there should be a regular time for japa and meditation, for no one knows when the auspicious moment will come. She continued: “It comes suddenly, one has no hint of it before hand.” A regular time keeps us at the door when the moment arrives. She also said, “God is realized only through His grace. Nonetheless, one must perform Japa and meditation, for they remove the impurities of one's mind.” Waiting for inspiration, for a quiet mind or for a festival day is not her advice. See slide 15.

QUESTION 14

A devotee hears that the Master worshipped Sarada Devi as the Divine Mother on the Phalaharini Kali Puja night of 1872. What does this tell us about where to look for the Mother?

A. The image is only a symbol, and a devotee should give it up in favour of living persons as soon as possible.
B. She can be found only in women who have renounced the world, since only they are fit to embody Her.
C. She is not only in the image: She can be recognised in a living person, and honoured in a woman near us.✓ Correct answer
D. It was a private ritual with no meaning beyond that one night, and no lesson for any devotee today.

He told her, “Think of her as the direct embodiment of the Divine Mother of the Universe,” and placed the fruits of his practice at her feet. Holy Mother said, “He left me behind this time for demonstrating that Motherhood to the world.” Note that this was a different night from the Kali Puja of Kartik. See slide 16.

QUESTION 15

A devotee finds the fierce form of Kali hard to approach, and recalls Holy Mother's dream during the Master's throat illness, in which Mother Kali stood with Her neck bent, because She had the same sore. What does the dream teach about the Mother of the universe?

A. That illness is a punishment which even the Mother cannot lift, and that it must simply be endured.
B. That the Master's illness was only an appearance and so of no real concern to the Mother of the universe.
C. That She shares our suffering closely, even one man's sore throat, and is not distant from pain.✓ Correct answer
D. That She was weary of the world and wished to withdraw from it, leaving Her children to themselves.

“I asked Her, ‘Mother, why do you stand like that?’ She replied, ‘It is because of this (pointing to the Master's sore in the throat); I too have it.’” For anyone who finds the fierce form hard to approach, this is the gentle answer of the tradition. See slide 17.

QUESTION 16

A person says: “I have done wrong things, so the Mother has no place for me.” How would Holy Mother answer?

A. She would say that only the pure may call themselves her children, and the rest must first make themselves clean through a long penance.
B. She is the mother of the virtuous as well as the wicked, though she still asks us to practise japa and meditation.✓ Correct answer
C. She would say that each child must earn liberation alone, since a mother cannot help with what one has done.
D. She would say that nothing is wrong, since actions have no weight, and the person need not think of it or of the lessons it may offer.

She said, “I am the mother of the virtuous as well as the wicked.” She also said, “Even God can do no harm to my children!” and, asked whether she was the mother of birds and animals, answered “Yes, of these also.” This is not permission to be careless: in the same conversations she insisted on japa and meditation, because they remove the impurities of the mind. See slide 18.

QUESTION 17

A reader notes that in “Kali the Mother” Swami Vivekananda wrote: “Who dares misery love, And hug the form of Death ... To him the Mother comes.” Years later he said, “I have proved it. For I have hugged the form of Death!” What is the fairest reading?

A. The Mother comes only to those who give up life itself, since She is found only where the world is left behind.
B. Meeting real suffering without flinching, and finding the Mother in it, is meeting Her; pain is not to be sought.✓ Correct answer
C. Suffering is unreal and need not be taken seriously, so the brave devotee simply ignores it and goes on as before.
D. One should go looking for misery in order to please Her, since the Mother comes to those who suffer most.

The poem asks us to worship the terrible as well as the sweet, since both are Hers. The reading above is our own interpretation of the lines and of his later remark. It does not say that pain is good, but that to meet it with courage and without pretending is to meet Her. See slide 19.

QUESTION 18 · MATCHING

Sort four moments of an ordinary day: does each belong to Vidya maya, the half of Her power that leads us home, or to Avidya maya, the half that scatters us among things?

1. A friend invites you to evening prayer and you go gladly.✓ Vidya maya
2. You scroll for an hour and forget even to say thank you.✓ Avidya maya
3. You feel the pull to put down an old grudge.✓ Vidya maya
4. You chase a bigger house in order to feel secure.✓ Avidya maya

“Mahamaya contains both Vidya-maya, the illusion of knowledge, and Avidya-maya, the illusion of ignorance.” Sri Ramakrishna named devotion, holy company and renunciation as Vidya maya, and the objects of the five senses that make one forget God as Avidya maya. Both are Hers: “Bondage and liberation are both of Her making.” Sorting a real day is a judgement, not a law, so notice where you would disagree. See slide 18.

Reflect: Which pulls in my day lead me towards Her, and which make me forget Her?

QUESTION 19 · MATCHING

The Shaiva tradition names four powers of the Mother working in us. Which power is at work in each situation?

1. “I did this. I am not good enough. I am the one who matters here.”✓ Khechari, the knower, the sense of “I”
2. “My thoughts race and scatter, and I cannot settle on one.”✓ Gochari, the inner organ: mind, intellect, ego
3. “My eyes and ears run after every new thing.”✓ Dikchari, the senses going out in all directions
4. “The things in my house and workplace keep pulling my attention.”✓ Bhuchari, the existing objects of the world

One power at four distances from us. The names and meanings are the Shaiva (Trika) tradition's teaching and are not in our three books. The practice is not to crush any of the four, since all four are Hers, but to align them with the goal so that they lead us home and do not bind us. See slide 19.

Reflect: Which of my four powers is least aligned with my goal just now, and what one change would align it?

QUESTION 20

You promised yourself a fixed minute of japa each day, but missed a whole week. Which advice from the class fits?

A. Give up the discipline, since a missed week shows that it is not suited to your temperament or your life.
B. Make up the missed week by doing it all in one long sitting, so that the account stays in balance and nothing is owed.
C. Wait until a feast day, so that the practice starts again with fresh energy and a clear intention for the weeks ahead.
D. Begin again at once, without self-reproach: the practice is the saying, whether or not the mind becomes steady.✓ Correct answer

Holy Mother: “Whether the mind becomes steady or not, practise Japa.” The line is said whether or not feeling arrives, and when the habit lapses we begin again, not with blame. The Study Companion adds the practical point that a missed day is information, not failure. See slide 20.

QUESTION 21

A family wants to keep Diwali in the spirit the class described. Which plan fits best?

A. A child making the house ready for its mother, perhaps by inviting someone who would otherwise be alone.✓ Correct answer
B. Keeping the festival only for those in the family who are devout, so that the worship stays undiluted by outsiders and by guests.
C. Lighting no lamp at all, since the Mother is beyond form and any outer light is only a distraction.
D. Lighting many lamps so that the Mother will protect the year's business and keep the household safe.

The spirit to avoid is worship as an insurance payment, a lamp lit so that the year will go well. The spirit to keep is a child making the house ready for its mother. Suggested practices for the days before the new moon include the lamp at dusk, japa, an offering before each action, and an evening review. See slide 21.

QUESTION 22

A devotee reading the prayer notices that its opening verse names the Mother as Kali and Bhadrakali, and in the same breath as Kshama the forgiving and Dhatri who upholds all. What does putting these together teach?

A. The fierce and the gentle are names of one Mother, so we do not have to choose which face to love.✓ Correct answer
B. They are separate goddesses to be worshipped one at a time, each at her own proper season of the year.
C. Kali is the angry face and Kshama the real face, so we should keep to the second and let the first go.
D. The gentle names are only for beginners who cannot yet face the fierce ones, and are later outgrown.

The verse is verse 2 of the Argala Stotram, recited before the Devi Mahatmya: “jayanti mangala kali bhadrakali kapalini | durga shiva kshama dhatri svaha svadha namo'stu te”. The same Mother is Jayanti, Mangala, Kali, Kapalini, Durga, Shiva, Kshama and Dhatri, and Sri Ramakrishna said of Her, “My Divine Mother is not only formless, She has forms as well.” See slide 22.

QUESTION 23

During the guided meditation you notice that for ten minutes you have been planning dinner. What does the class advise?

A. Stop the sitting, since the mind will not settle today and forcing it only builds a habit of restlessness.
B. Scold the mind firmly so that it learns not to wander, since discipline is built through strictness.
C. Bring the mind back without comment: the returning is itself the practice, and not a sign of failure.✓ Correct answer
D. Count the breath harder and faster, so that no thought can find room to return during the sitting, as a firmer discipline.

“When the mind wanders, and it will, bring it back without comment.” In the last part of the sitting the image is the Mother's lap: a child asleep there is not holding on, it has been picked up. Nothing has to be achieved, and nothing has to be felt. See slide 23.

QUESTION 24

You keep the evening review and tonight you cannot recall seeing the Mother anywhere in the day. How should this be read?

A. As a reason to invent a moment of devotion, so that the page is not left blank at the end of the day.
B. As proof that the practice is not working, and so a reason to drop the review and try something else.
C. As a sign that you must have done something wrong that day, and should look hard for what it was.
D. As information, not failure: note it, offer the day, and set one thing to look for tomorrow morning.✓ Correct answer

The three lines are: where I saw Her, where I forgot Her, and one thing for tomorrow. Said before sleep, then the day is handed back: “Done, and offered.” A blank in the record tells you where to look tomorrow; it is not a verdict. See slide 24.

QUESTION 25

A student is troubled that she dislikes the fierce form of Kali. She reads Swami Vivekananda's words about his early years, “How I used to hate Kali! ... But I had to accept Her at last!” What can she fairly learn from this?

A. That honest struggle with the fierce form is no barrier to Her; one need not pretend to love Her first.✓ Correct answer
B. That sensitive people are better off avoiding the fierce form altogether, since struggle only does harm.
C. That argument alone, if clever enough, settles the matter, so one need only reason one's way to Her.
D. That acceptance came only because he was forced against his will, so it was not a real acceptance.

He also said, “She made a slave of me.” The lesson is our own reading: his refusal was honest and his acceptance came in the end, so resistance is not proof that the way is wrong. The struggle is told more fully in the Study Companion. See slide 25.

Reflect: What do I resist that may be Her teaching me?

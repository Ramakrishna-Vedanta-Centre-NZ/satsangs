The question

We worship her in the image. Where else does she live?

1

2

In the universe: the one Power behind all that exists

3

In us: as body, breath, mind, intellect and the sense of “I”

NOTES

Why call the ultimate Reality “Mother”? In the Indian spiritual tradition she is the one who gives birth to the universe, nourishes and protects it, and finally draws it back into herself. Above all, a mother is approachable: a child needs no qualification to run to her.

The image worshipped in the Durgā Pūjā is a doorway, not a limit. This satsang walks through that doorway in three steps: from the Mother on the altar, to the Power behind the whole cosmos, to the same Power living as our own body, breath and mind.

ONE WITH BRAHMAN

Fire and its power to burn

“Brahman and Śakti are identical. If you accept the one, you must accept the other. It is like fire and its power to burn.”

Sri Ramakrishna, The Gospel of Sri Ramakrishna

At rest

Brahman

When he thought of the Supreme as inactive, neither creating, preserving nor destroying, Sri Ramakrishna called it Brahman.

In action

Śakti, the Mother

When he thought of it as active, creating, preserving and destroying, he called it Śakti: the same Reality, now as Power.

NOTES

Brahman at rest, the Mother in motion: one Reality. Sri Ramakrishna used simple images: fire and its power to burn, milk and its whiteness, a gem and its lustre. We cannot think of one without the other.

He also spoke of a snake, coiled and still, and the same snake moving. Advaita Vedanta calls the one Reality Brahman; the worshipper calls it Śakti, the Mother. Kashmir Śaivism says the same: Śiva and Śakti, light and its pulse (spanda), are one.

ONE WITH BRAHMAN

“I am the Queen”: the Devī Sūkta

Ṛg Veda 10.125.1

अहं रुद्रेभिर्वसुभिश्चराम्यहमादित्यैरुत विश्वदेवैः

ahaṃ rudrebhir vasubhiś carāmy aham ādityair uta viśvadevaiḥ

“I move with the Rudras and the Vasus, with the Ādityas and all the gods.”

She is the power within every god.

Ṛg Veda 10.125.3

अहं राष्ट्री सङ्गमनी वसूनां चिकितुषी प्रथमा यज्ञियानाम्

ahaṃ rāṣṭrī saṅgamanī vasūnāṃ cikituṣī prathamā yajñiyānām

“I am the sovereign Queen, gatherer of treasures, the Knower, first of those worthy of worship.”

She rules, she gives, she knows.

Ṛg Veda 10.125.8

अहमेव वात इव प्र वाम्यारभमाणा भुवनानि विश्वा

aham eva vāta iva pra vāmy ārabhamāṇā bhuvanāni viśvā

“I breathe forth like the wind, giving shape to all the worlds.”

Her breath is the life of the universe.

God as Mother is as old as the Veda itself.

NOTES

Ṛg Veda 10.125, the Devī Sūkta, is spoken by the seer Vāk, daughter of the sage Ambhṛṇa. Having realised her oneness with the Goddess, she speaks in the first person as the Goddess herself. It is traditionally recited together with the Chaṇḍī.

For newcomers: the idea of God as Mother is not a later addition. It is among the oldest scriptural voices of the Indian spiritual tradition. She is not one deity among many but the power within all the gods: the knower, the giver, and the breath of the worlds.

SHE HAS BECOME ALL

One Power, many forms

THE MACROCOSM

Galaxies, atoms, cells, breath and thought: she has become all of it.

सर्वं खल्विदं ब्रह्म

“All this is indeed Brahman.” Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.1

1

Modern physics

Matter and energy are interchangeable (E = mc²). What looks solid is energy in concentrated form.

2

Swami Vivekananda

In Rāja Yoga he taught that all matter comes from ākāśa and every force from prāṇa, both arising from one cosmic source.

3

Vedanta

That one energy is not blind. It is conscious, it is Śakti, and we call her Mother.

NOTES

An analogy, not a proof. Modern physics describes matter as concentrated energy; the seers saw every form as Śakti condensed into name and form (nāma-rūpa). Swami Vivekananda taught that all matter comes from ākāśa and all force from prāṇa, both arising from the cosmic mind.

Example: ice, water and steam look different, yet they are one substance. A galaxy, a body and a thought look different, yet they are one Power. The Upaniṣad says it simply: sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ brahma, all this is indeed Brahman.

SHE HAS BECOME ALL

Three moods of the one Mother

1

Mahākālī

महाकाली

The story: Madhu and Kaiṭabha, born while Viṣṇu sleeps

Within us: inertia, dullness, spiritual sleep (tamas)

Her grace: she wakes us up

2

Mahālakṣmī

महालक्ष्मी

The story: Mahiṣāsura, the buffalo demon who keeps changing shape

Within us: restless desire, anger and ego (rajas)

Her grace: strength to conquer ourselves

3

Mahāsarasvatī

महासरस्वती

The story: Śumbha and Niśumbha, the proud brothers

Within us: subtle pride, the sense of “I” and “mine”

Her grace: wisdom that dissolves duality

The battle of the Chaṇḍī is fought within us, and the Mother fights it for us.

NOTES

The Devī Māhātmya (Chaṇḍī), seven hundred verses from the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, tells three stories, each presided over by one aspect of the Mother: Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī. It is recited during Durgā Pūjā and Navarātri.

A common inner reading sees the demons as tendencies of the mind, matching the three guṇas: inertia, restlessness and subtle pride. The outer festival pictures an inner victory. We cannot win it by effort alone: we call on her, and she fights for us.

KASHMIR ŚAIVISM

Citi: the free Power behind all

Sūtra 1

चितिः स्वतन्त्रा विश्वसिद्धिहेतुः ॥ १ ॥

citiḥ svatantrā viśva-siddhi-hetuḥ

“Consciousness, perfectly free, is the cause of the whole universe.”

Citi is feminine: the Mother as pure, self-aware Power. She needs nothing outside herself to create.

Sūtra 2

स्वेच्छया स्वभित्तौ विश्वमुन्मीलयति ॥ २ ॥

svecchayā svabhittau viśvam unmīlayati

“By her own will, on her own screen, she unfolds the universe.”

No outside material and no outside canvas: like a dream within the dreamer, the world appears within her.

She is both the painter and the canvas.

NOTES

The Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam (“The Heart of Recognition”) is a short text of twenty sūtras by Kṣemarāja, a great teacher of Kashmir Śaivism. Its first sūtra names Citi, free Consciousness, as the one cause of the universe. The word is feminine: Citi is the Mother.

Sūtra 2 adds that she needs no outside material: she unfolds the world by her own will, on her own screen. In a dream, the dreamer’s mind is stage, actors and scenery at once. This echoes Sri Ramakrishna: Brahman and Śakti are one, like fire and its power to burn.

KASHMIR ŚAIVISM

She contracts to become us

Sūtra 3

तन्नाना अनुरूपग्राह्यग्राहकभेदात्

tan nānā anurūpa-grāhya-grāhaka-bhedāt

“The universe is many because she divides into matching knowers and known.”

Seer and seen arise together, like two hands of one body.

Sūtra 4

चितिसंकोचात्मा चेतनोऽपि संकुचितविश्वमयः

citi-saṅkocātmā cetano ’pi saṅkucita-viśvamayaḥ

“Even the individual, being Consciousness contracted, holds the universe in contracted form.”

Each of us is the whole of her, compressed: a bubble holding the ocean’s nature.

Sūtra 5

चितिरेव चेतनपदादवरूढा चेत्यसंकोचिनी चित्तम्

citir eva cetana-padād avarūḍhā cetya-saṅkocinī cittam

“Citi herself, descending from pure awareness and narrowing to what she perceives, becomes the mind.”

Our mind is the Mother in disguise.

From Citi to citta, and back: sādhanā expands the mind into her again.

NOTES

Sūtra 3: one Consciousness appears as many, because she divides herself into matching knowers and known things. Sūtra 4: each individual is Citi contracted, holding the whole universe in seed form, as a seed holds the tree.

Sūtra 5 completes the descent: Citi herself, narrowing to what she perceives, becomes citta, the mind. So the mind is not an enemy but the Mother in a narrowed form. Sādhanā is the return journey, and the five sheaths show its stages.

SRI RAMAKRISHNA’S TEACHING

Her two powers: Vidyā Māyā and Avidyā Māyā

अविद्या माया

Avidyā Māyā

The power that binds: it pulls us outward

Attachment to sense pleasures and possessions

The sense of “I and mine”

Forgetting who we really are

Like a mist, it hides the sun without harming it

विद्या माया

Vidyā Māyā

The power that frees: it leads us inward

Discrimination (viveka) and dispassion

Devotion, compassion and the company of the holy

Longing for God, prayer and service

Like a ladder, it carries us up to the roof

Both are her powers. The same Mother who binds is the Mother who sets free.

NOTES

Sri Ramakrishna taught that Māyā, the Mother’s power, has two faces. Avidyā Māyā deludes: it binds us through lust and greed, the restless pull of the senses, and the sense of “I and mine”. Vidyā Māyā liberates: knowledge, devotion, dispassion, discrimination, compassion and holy company draw us towards God.

The Upaniṣad says the mind alone is the cause of bondage and liberation (mana eva manuṣyāṇāṃ kāraṇaṃ bandha-mokṣayoḥ). The same energy of the mind binds when it runs outward and frees when it turns inward. We take refuge in Vidyā Māyā, and the Mother herself then takes us beyond both.

Bhūcarī

moves in existence: the objects of the outer world

तदपरिज्ञाने स्वशक्तिभिर्व्यामोहितता संसारित्वम् ॥ १२ ॥

Not recognising her, we are deluded by our own powers: this is saṃsāra.

तत्परिज्ञाने चित्तमेव अन्तर्मुखीभावेन चेतनपदाध्यारोहात् चितिः ॥ १३ ॥

Recognising her, the mind turns inward, rises, and becomes Citi again.

NOTES

The same four powers bind or free, depending on which way she turns them. When she plays her līlā, she turns them outward, hides her true nature, and the jīva is deluded by its own powers.

When she is gracious, she turns them inward: the mind (citta) rises back to Consciousness (citi), and she reveals herself. Practice: in any moment of seeing or thinking, recall “this too is her power”.

Each subtler sheath pervades the denser ones, as space pervades a room. Beyond the five is Parāśakti, the cosmic Power one with Consciousness, flowing in and energising every layer. Meditation retraces the path inward: body, breath, mind, intellect, bliss, and into her.

OUR SPIRITUAL TRADITION

Dakshineswar: a child and his Mother

HIS FIRST VISION

When his longing became unbearable, the temple, the doors, everything vanished. He saw a boundless, shining ocean of Consciousness, its waves rushing towards him, and the Mother’s presence filled him.

From the life of Sri Ramakrishna, as he later described it

1

Longing

As priest of Mā Bhavatāriṇī, he wept each evening that another day had passed without seeing her.

2

A child’s trust

He spoke to Mā as a child speaks to its mother: simply, trustingly, depending on her for everything.

3

Bhajan

A song of Rāmprasād, so dear to Sri Ramakrishna: [title to be chosen]

NOTES

The ocean he saw is the same Parāśakti in which we are all bubbles. Sri Ramakrishna served as priest of the Kālī temple at Dakshineswar from 1856. His first vision of the Mother came after intense longing, as he later told his disciples.

He related to Mā as a child to its mother, with complete trust and simplicity. The lesson for us is longing: he taught that if we cry for her as a child cries for its mother, she cannot stay away. Devotional songs, like those of Rāmprasād, nourish that longing.

OUR SPIRITUAL TRADITION

Ṣoḍaśī Pūjā: the Mother in Ma Sarada

यथाग्नेर्दाहिका शक्तिः रामकृष्णे स्थिता हि या ।
सर्वविद्यास्वरूपां तां शारदां प्रणमाम्यहम् ॥

yathāgner dāhikā śaktiḥ rāmakṛṣṇe sthitā hi yā
sarva-vidyā-svarūpāṃ tāṃ śāradāṃ praṇamāmy aham

“As the power to burn abides in fire, she abides in Ramakrishna. To Sarada, the embodiment of all knowledge, I bow.”

1872

On the night of Phalahāriṇī Kālī Pūjā, Sri Ramakrishna worshipped Sri Sarada Devi as the Divine Mother, Ṣoḍaśī.

His offering

At the end of the worship he offered at her feet the fruits of all his spiritual practices.

Every woman is Mā

He saw all women as manifestations of the Divine Mother, and taught his disciples to do the same.

Holy Mother: “I am the mother of the wicked, as I am the mother of the virtuous.”

NOTES

In 1872, on the night of Phalahāriṇī Kālī Pūjā at Dakshineswar, Sri Ramakrishna worshipped Sri Sarada Devi as Ṣoḍaśī, the Divine Mother. Her praṇām mantra uses his own image: as the power to burn abides in fire, she is the Śakti abiding in him.

Holy Mother’s motherhood embraced everyone: monks and householders, the learned and the simple, saints and sinners. In her the Divine Mother became approachable, practical and kind. Seeing every woman as Mā is a direct practice of this teaching.

OUR SPIRITUAL TRADITION

Belur Math, 1901: Swamiji worships the Mother

1

The first Durgā Pūjā

In October 1901 Swami Vivekananda celebrated Durgā Pūjā at Belur Math, with Holy Mother’s blessing and without animal sacrifice.

2

A living tradition

The worship continues every year at Belur Math and in Ramakrishna centres around the world, including our own in New Zealand.

3

Śakti and society

Swamiji saw the worship of Śakti as inseparable from honouring, educating and empowering women.

KALI THE MOTHER

Who dares misery love,
And hug the form of Death,
Dance in Destruction’s dance,
To him the Mother comes.

Swami Vivekananda, Kashmir, 1898

He worshipped her in the image, in Holy Mother, and in the strength she gives.

NOTES

Swami Vivekananda began the Durgā Pūjā at Belur Math in 1901, with Holy Mother’s blessing, so that the Math would worship the Mother as Sri Ramakrishna had. The worship continues every year at Belur Math and in Ramakrishna centres around the world.

He wrote “Kali the Mother” in Kashmir in 1898, after an overwhelming experience of the Mother as the terrible and the beautiful in one. For Swamiji, worship of Śakti was inseparable from strength, fearlessness, and the honouring and education of women.

LIVING IT

Paths to the Mother

1

Japa

Repeat her name, or simply “Mā”, with feeling. Holy Mother taught that the Name clears the mind as wind clears the clouds.

2

Stotra and bhajan

Chant the Chaṇḍī or sing to her. Sound and feeling together turn the whole mind towards her.

3

Seeing Mā in all

Greet every woman, and every being, as a form of the Mother. Service becomes worship.

4

Meditation

Feel her presence as a soft light in the heart, filling body, breath and mind.

5

Surrender

Śaraṇāgati: “Mother, I am your child. Do as you will.” Let her carry the burden.

GRACE

Sri Ramakrishna: the breeze of grace is always blowing. We need only unfurl our sails.

NOTES

These five paths suit different temperaments, and most of us combine two or three. Japa and meditation can be practised anywhere. Stotras and bhajans turn feeling towards her. Seeing Mā in all turns every meeting into worship.

Surrender (śaraṇāgati) is the child’s prayer: “Mother, I am your child; do as you will.” All five are ways of unfurling the sail: effort prepares us, and her grace carries us. Sri Ramakrishna and Holy Mother both stressed regular practice done with love.

LIVING IT

Her grace in everyday action

“If you want peace of mind, do not find fault with others. Rather see your own faults. Learn to make the whole world your own. No one is a stranger, my child; the whole world is your own.”

Holy Mother Sri Sarada Devi, her last message

Morning

Offer the day: “Mā, may all I do be your worship”

Salute her in the first breath and the first meal

Bow inwardly to the first person you see

During the day

Before each task: “Mā, you do it through me”

See her in one person you meet

When upset, pause: this too is her energy

Evening

Offer back the day, successes and slips

Thank her for one moment of grace

Fall asleep resting in her

NOTES

Spiritual practice and everyday work are not two separate worlds. Cooking, driving, emails and caring for family can all be offered to her and done by her power. Holy Mother’s last message shows the heart of this: see your own faults, and make the whole world your own.

Use the checklist gently. Begin with two or three items for a week, then add more. A missed day is information, not failure: simply begin again with the morning offering. Over time the offering becomes natural, and the day itself becomes worship.

Guided meditation: the Mother within

1

Sit steady. Eyes closed; head, neck and chest relaxed and upright.

2

Feel the breath. Silently: “Each breath, she breathes me.”

3

The body. Feel it as her most contracted form, held in her power.

4

Move outward. Breath, feeling, thought, the sense of “I”: “This too is You, Mā.”

5

The heart. See her as a soft golden light, filling body and mind.

6

Rest in her. Let the light spread beyond the body, into her boundless ocean.

7

Offer. Offer the meditation for the welfare of all; open the eyes slowly.

Breathe with the light

NOTES About ten minutes. Breathe in as the light grows and out as it settles. Move inward through the sheaths, recognising each as her. If the mind wanders, return gently: every return is practice. Close by offering the meditation for the welfare of all.
